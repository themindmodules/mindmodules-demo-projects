# 3-minute pitch (6 slides, 30 seconds each)

**Slide 1: Hook (problem).**
"Last semester our class QR code was in the WhatsApp group within 40 seconds. Half the people who
'attended' that lecture were in the hostel. The teacher knew, and could do nothing about it."
(Use your own real story if you have one. If not, use a teacher's quote from the venue.)

**Slide 2: Why existing tools miss it.**
"Roll call wastes 8 minutes. Paper gets signed for friends. A fixed QR code is a photo away from
useless. And GPS apps are creepy and easy to fake."

**Slide 3: Our solution.**
"RollCall: a code that changes every 15 seconds, one registered phone per student, and seven simple
rules that tell the teacher exactly which scans look wrong, and why."

**Slide 4: Live demo (the most important slide).**
Open the dashboard. Point at the class grid, then at the flags table. Say:
"This is a simulated lecture of 60 students. We planted six cheating attempts. Here, one phone scanned
for three people. Here, a screenshot scanned 74 seconds after it was shown. Here, last week's code.
All six were blocked, and all 49 honest students were marked. One honest student with a new phone
shows as 'review', and the teacher clears that in five seconds."
(Be clear the data is simulated. If you have a real scanning page running, scan it live instead.)

**Slide 5: How it works and why it is simple.**
The architecture diagram, then the rule table. "Every flag comes with a sentence. No black box,
no location tracking, no face data."

**Slide 6: Roadmap and ask.**
"Next: a scanning web page, a pilot with one class for two weeks, and an optional in-room check for
exam-critical lectures. We need one teacher willing to pilot it."

## Delivery tips

- Rehearse the demo three times; run it from `file://` so venue Wi-Fi cannot break it.
- Say "simulated" before anyone asks.
- Do not quote cheating percentages you cannot source.
